Building on Costa Rica's Pacific coast is different from building almost anywhere else. Between the salt air, the tropical humidity and a demanding permitting process, a successful project is not an accident — it's the result of five things done right. Get them right from the beginning and you protect your budget, your timeline and the value of your property for decades.

What Makes Construction on the Pacific Coast Different?

From Jacó to Manuel Antonio, coastal construction faces conditions few other regions share: salt-laden breezes that corrode unprotected metal, heavy seasonal rains that test every roof and foundation, and municipalities with strict review processes. A build that ignores these realities ends up with leaks, rust and year-long delays. One that respects them delivers a structure you can rely on for a lifetime.

The 5 Keys to a Successful Construction Project

These five keys guide every project we run — and they are the same ones you should expect from any contractor you hire.

1. Plan Before You Dig

A clear plan is the difference between a smooth build and a frustrating one. It starts with site analysis — soil conditions, drainage and orientation relative to the sun and the sea breeze — and ends with a detailed set of plans and a realistic schedule. At MA Soluciones we lock the scope, timeline and budget into a written plan before we move a shovelful of earth, so surprises are caught in drawings, not halfway through construction.

2. Secure Your Permits Early

Construction in Costa Rica requires permits from the municipality and, in many cases, revisions from the ICE and the structural, mechanical and electrical reviews of the Colegio Federado de Ingenieros y de Arquitectos (CFIA). Waiting until the last minute stalls your build and exposes you to fines. An experienced contractor manages this paperwork for you — in parallel with procurement — so the heaviest bureaucratic step never holds up the project.

3. Choose Materials Built for the Coast

The Pacific climate is unforgiving: salt, humidity and UV radiation degrade ordinary materials quickly. We specify stainless or hot-dip galvanized hardware, corrosion-resistant fasteners, marine-grade finishes, and roofing and paints rated for tropical exposure. Spending a little more on the right materials at the start saves far more on repairs a few years later — it is the cheapest insurance your project will ever have.

4. Control the Budget From the Start

Cost overruns are usually a planning problem, not a construction problem. We work with transparent, itemized budgets that separate materials, labor and permits, with clear allowances for the variables common in coastal builds. Regular cost reviews during the project keep every decision visible, so you always know where your money is going and what remains in reserve. When a change is worth making, you decide it — with the numbers in front of you.

5. Supervise Every Phase

The best plans fall apart without supervision. Quality control means checking foundations before columns are poured, verifying waterproofing before the roof goes on, and inspecting every finish before handover. Since 2010 our founder Robert Matarrita has personally supervised projects across the Central Pacific — with more than 300 projects delivered and a 98% client satisfaction rate, we treat every stage as a checkpoint, not an afterthought.
